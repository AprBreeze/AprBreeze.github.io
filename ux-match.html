Match - AI Job Search

Rebuilding the most repetitive stage of the job hunt around your resume.

Role Product Designer and Developer
Duration 2 weeks
Team Solo Builder
Methods Spec-driven design, Vibe-coding prototyping, Agentic engineering

Quick Summary

Match rebuilds the search stage of a job hunt: the posting-after-posting reading, and the guessing about whether you qualify.

It starts from a resume, suggests titles across the job lanes that resume can evidence, and returns a shortlist that keeps the model's best guess separate from everything the filters set aside. I built it end to end as a solo project, from the flow and the copy to the specs and the working prototype.

Where this started

As a new grad, my job hunt meant jumping between LinkedIn, Indeed, and a handful of other boards.

Reading posting after posting, guessing by eye whether I qualified. Hours of repetitive work, taken from the time I wanted for learning and growing.

I couldn't fix the whole hunt, so I fixed its most repetitive stage: searching for and filtering jobs.

Start from what you already have

Resume is the only entry. Everyone has one.

No search bar, no query crafting, no keyword guessing. PDF import is parsed to text the model reads reliably, so output quality doesn't depend on how you formatted your file. Paste is there, but tucked behind a link so the common path stays one click.

Jobs you could apply to

Most people are capable of roles they would never think to search.

The tool reads the resume and proposes titles across the job markets it can evidence, including adjacent lanes you didn't know existed. You keep control: toggle what fits, add your own, cap at three.

One optimized path

All real configuration stays in the CLI and a separate test page.

The product shows only the flow that works: no scores, ranks, LLM toggles, or fetch counts. I tuned against the test surface to find the balanced configuration, then shipped users the result of that tuning instead of the controls.

Screenshot placeholder The product flow, or the Settings modal

Filter cheap first

Running the model over every posting would burn tokens and hurt output quality.

Too much context in one call invites hallucination. So deterministic gates run first, on title, years of experience, and board-level signals. Only the surviving shortlist reaches the model for ranking.

Screenshot placeholder The pipeline: fetch, gates, shortlist, model rank, human pick

Results that admit uncertainty

Best Match is the model's shortlist. Backup Jobs holds everything the filters set aside.

Backup exists because filters can be wrong, and a wrong filter shouldn't hide a role you could have landed. Each row is title, company, location, board, and one plain sentence on why.

Screenshot placeholder The results list with both tabs and the why lines

Open source, your own key

I want this to be a tool people can adapt, so every AI step sits behind one transport.

It runs against a local CLI or any OpenAI-compatible API, with your own key. No vendor lock-in, and no shared service holding your resume.

What I learned

The specs, the decision log, and the documents to orchestrate agents came from studying agent engineering and rebuilding my workflow around it.

Two resources shaped it most: Google and Kaggle's 5-Day AI Agents: Intensive Vibe Coding Course, and Kun's Agentic Engineering workflow talk.

That pushed me to try real vibe-coded prototyping this time, moving fast on flow and copy while keeping the reasoning in specs I wrote and reviewed.

I'm grateful that today's LLM advances let designers take this on themselves: framing the problem, designing the flow, and carrying it through to a working implementation, all at the prototype stage.

The project isn't done. Some job boards are only partly supported. I'll keep updating until they are, then share some more updates.
